## Current mystery canon — supersedes the earlier innocent-Bram resolution

Bram secretly removes and sells timber under his care to pay a debt, including material supporting the old crossing. He recognises the danger and diverts travellers to conceal his work. His affection towards Poppy is real, but he exploits it to avoid scrutiny. Poppy and Willow later prove a lie by connecting separated details, discover the removal was deliberate, and expose his attempt to blame the storm after rescuing him. His rescue effort does not erase his responsibility; he loses his caretaker position. Physical layout, timings and witness access must be established before students can make the later deductions. The Chapter 1 light, scraping, shavings, cuff and bell plant evidence without revealing these answers.

The earlier character bible and beat outline below are retained as development history. Bram’s innocence, the chest/rope explanation for the scraping, and the purely helpful map alteration are superseded.

## Final chapter continuity (22 October 2026)

All six chapters now have manuscript readers. Chapters 5 and 6 replace the earlier innocent-Bram rescue draft; the former chest-only explanation and warning-light ending are no longer canon. Chapter 1 prose and approved tasks remain unchanged.

- The mill and cottage stand on the village bank. Alder Arch is the high stone bridge connecting the two hillsides. The signal rock is on firm ground before the fallen beech limb. Adults clear that obstruction before crossing the arch.
- The mender and apprentice meet Bram at the far approach during the afternoon. He diverts them towards the arch. They find that approach obstructed, wait in a wayside shed, and return to the lower bridge at night. The mender hurts her ankle on its approach.
- Bram removes and sells timber, including crossing braces. His attempted concealment includes shaving repair marks from timber and dragging the chest in front of it. The shavings alone do not identify a source; the marked timber and cut brace supply corroborating physical evidence.
- At quarter to twelve the rabbits see the moving light and hear scraping. Bram subsequently hears the travellers, takes the rope, moves the oil lamp downstairs, marks the map and starts the warning. He leaves before finishing it and crosses before the planks fail.
- Chapter 4 fixes the first witnessed bell just after midnight and the later one after quarter past twelve. Chapter 5 establishes deliberate signalling through a copied pattern before anyone identifies the shelter occupants.
- Bram uses the rope as a handhold up the slippery slope. The rescue party brings poles and canvas. Poppy and the baker stay at the signal rock; Willow takes the map from the ferryman and accompanies the adults.
- Willow extinguished her paper lantern beyond the shed before the narrow path. It was crushed during the bank incident, relit in Chapter 5, damaged on the return and repaired by the apprentice in Chapter 6.
- At the shelter Bram denies visiting the crossing since the previous morning. The mender contradicts this. Back at the bakery, the rabbits question his account before adults inspect the timber and the bridge from firm ground.
- Bram admits the sales and concealment after the independent evidence is assembled. He loses access immediately and his position later that week. The ending preserves relief, kindness and the rabbits’ friendship without treating rescue as exoneration.
