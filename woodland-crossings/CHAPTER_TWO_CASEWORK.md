## 6. An unexpected movement

Poppy had recognised the movement of a branch. She still jumped when the reeds rustled.

Use “yet” to join the sentences and emphasise the unexpected contrast. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.

yet. Poppy had recognised the movement of a branch, yet she still jumped when the reeds rustled.
no-comma. Poppy had recognised the movement of a branch yet she still jumped when the reeds rustled.
splice. Poppy had recognised the movement of a branch, she still jumped when the reeds rustled.

**Answer:** yet

Recognising one harmless movement has not stopped Poppy reacting fearfully to another. A comma comes before “yet”.

## 7. Evidence for a suspicion

Poppy suspected that the bell had left the circular mark. A notch interrupted its edge.

Use “for” to join the sentences, introducing the evidence for Poppy’s suspicion. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.

splice. Poppy suspected that the bell had left the circular mark, a notch interrupted its edge.
no-comma. Poppy suspected that the bell had left the circular mark for a notch interrupted its edge.
for. Poppy suspected that the bell had left the circular mark, for a notch interrupted its edge.

**Answer:** for

The notch supports Poppy’s suspicion because she remembers the chip in the bell’s lip. It supports the idea without proving it. A comma comes before the conjunction “for”.

## 8. Spot the comma splice

Poppy checks four versions of the same notebook observation.

Which sentence contains a comma splice?

A. Although the rain had softened the bootprints, the sheltered circular marks were still clear.
B. The rain had softened the bootprints, the sheltered circular marks were still clear.
C. The rain had softened the bootprints, but the sheltered circular marks were still clear.
D. The rain had softened the bootprints; the sheltered circular marks were still clear.

**Answer:** B

B joins two complete clauses with only a comma. In A, “although” makes the opening dependent. C uses a comma with “but”. D uses a semicolon.

## 9. Which way does the reasoning run?

Poppy suspected that the bell had left the circular mark, so a notch interrupted its edge.

Why is “so” wrong here?

A. A coordinating conjunction cannot follow a comma.
B. The notch is evidence for Poppy’s suspicion, rather than a result of her suspicion.
C. The second clause has no subject.
D. “So” can only introduce a future action.

**Answer:** B

Poppy’s suspicion did not create the notch. She noticed the notch and used it as evidence. “For” expresses that relationship: Poppy suspected that the bell had left the circular mark, for a notch interrupted its edge.
